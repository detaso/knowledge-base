Visibility controls who can read your published knowledge base content. You can make your entire workspace open to the public internet, lock it down to signed-in team members only, or combine both approaches by keeping the workspace public while marking sensitive individual articles as internal. Understanding how these settings interact helps you share the right content with the right audience without accidental exposure.

## Workspace-Level Visibility

The workspace visibility setting is the master switch that controls the default access level for all published content.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Public" icon="globe">
    Anyone on the internet can read your published articles without creating an account or signing in. This is the right choice for customer-facing documentation, public help centers, and open developer docs.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Private" icon="lock">
    Only workspace members who are signed in can read articles. Visitors who are not authenticated see a login prompt instead of your content. Use this for internal team wikis, HR documentation, or any proprietary knowledge.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

To change your workspace visibility setting:

1. Go to **Settings > Workspace**.
2. Scroll to the **Visibility** section.
3. Select **Public** or **Private**.
4. Click **Save Changes**.

The change takes effect immediately for all readers.

## Per-Article Visibility

Even when your workspace is set to **Public**, you can restrict individual articles to signed-in workspace members only. This lets you maintain a mixed knowledge base — most content is public, but internal drafts, HR policies, or pricing details stay private.

To set visibility on a specific article:

1. Open the article in the editor.
2. In the right-hand metadata panel, find the **Visibility** field.
3. Choose **Public** (inherits workspace setting) or **Workspace-only** (always requires sign-in, regardless of workspace setting).
4. Save or publish the article.

Per-article visibility overrides the workspace setting in one direction only: you can make a public-workspace article more restrictive, but you cannot make a private-workspace article publicly accessible to unauthenticated readers.

## Password Protection

On the **Pro plan**, you can protect your entire knowledge base with a shared password. Visitors are prompted to enter the password before viewing any content, regardless of whether individual articles are set to Public or Workspace-only.

To enable password protection:

1. Go to **Settings > Workspace > Visibility**.
2. Toggle on **Password Protection**.
3. Enter and confirm your chosen password.
4. Click **Save Changes**.

Share the password directly with the people who need access. To change or remove the password, return to the same settings page at any time.

<Warning>
  Before switching your workspace from Private to Public, review your article list carefully. Any article marked **Public** will become immediately readable by anyone on the internet. Use the **Content** page filtered by visibility to audit your articles before making the switch.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Draft articles are never visible to readers regardless of workspace or article visibility settings. Only published articles are surfaced to readers. You can safely draft and iterate on content without worrying about accidental exposure.
</Note>
