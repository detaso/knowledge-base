Your knowledge base gives you layered control over who can access, contribute to, and manage your content. Permissions operate at two levels: **workspace roles** define what each team member can do inside the editor, and **visibility settings** on articles and categories control what external readers and signed-in members can see. Understanding both layers lets you build a knowledge base that's open where it needs to be and protected where it doesn't.

## Workspace Roles

Every member of your workspace is assigned one of three roles. Roles are set per member and apply across the entire workspace unless overridden by category-level access rules.

| Role | Description | Capabilities |
|------|-------------|--------------|
| **Admin** | Full control over the workspace | Create, edit, publish, and delete any article or category; manage team members and roles; configure branding, domain, and integrations; access analytics and audit logs |
| **Editor** | Content creation and management | Create and edit articles; move articles between categories; submit articles for review; publish articles (if publishing rights are enabled for editors); view drafts and review-stage content |
| **Viewer** | Read-only access | Read published articles and browse categories within the workspace; cannot create, edit, or delete content; cannot see drafts or articles in review |

<Info>
  Workspace roles are assigned by Admins. If you need to change a team member's role or add new members to your workspace, see [Managing Team Roles](/admin/roles).
</Info>

### Who Can Publish

By default, only Admins can publish articles from the Review state to Published. You can change this in your workspace settings to allow Editors to publish their own work — useful for smaller teams or content areas that don't require formal approval. Admins always retain the ability to publish regardless of this setting.

## Article Visibility

Each article has a visibility setting that controls who can read it, independent of the article's publish status. You set visibility when creating or editing an article.

<CardGroup cols={1}>
  <Card title="Public" icon="globe">
    Anyone with access to your knowledge base URL can read the article, including visitors who are not signed in. Use this for help documentation, onboarding guides, and other content intended for the broadest possible audience.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Workspace-Only" icon="lock">
    Only signed-in workspace members can read the article. It does not appear in search results or navigation for unauthenticated visitors. Use this for internal processes, HR policies, team-specific runbooks, and any content that should stay within your organization.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Draft (Editors Only)" icon="pencil">
    The article is in progress and is only visible to workspace members with Editor or Admin access. Drafts never appear in public navigation or search results, regardless of any other setting. This state is the default for all newly created articles.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Warning>
  If your knowledge base is in public mode, articles default to **Public** visibility unless you explicitly change them to Workspace-only. Always review the visibility setting before publishing internal documentation — sensitive operational docs, employee information, and confidential processes must be set to Workspace-only to prevent unintended public exposure.
</Warning>

## Category-Level Access

In addition to article-level visibility, you can restrict entire categories to specific roles or teams. Category-level access rules apply to every article within that category, overriding article-level settings where they conflict.

### How Category Restrictions Work

When you restrict a category to a specific role (for example, Admin-only), readers without that role will not see the category in the sidebar, breadcrumbs, or search results. The articles inside an Admin-only category behave as if they are workspace-only even if their individual visibility is set to Public.

### Setting Up Category Restrictions

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Categories manager">
    Go to **Content** → **Categories** in your workspace dashboard. Find the category you want to restrict.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open category settings">
    Click the three-dot menu next to the category name and select **Category Settings**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the minimum role">
    Under **Access Control**, select the minimum role required to view this category. Options are Viewer (default), Editor, or Admin. Members below the minimum role will not see the category at all.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save and verify">
    Save your changes. Open an incognito window or sign in with a lower-privilege test account to confirm the category is no longer visible to unauthorized readers.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Category access restrictions are additive — restricting a parent category does not automatically restrict its child categories. Set access rules explicitly on each category you want to protect, or check the **Apply to all subcategories** option when saving your category settings to propagate the restriction downward.
</Note>
