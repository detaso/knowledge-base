Your knowledge base includes built-in full-text search that lets readers find relevant articles instantly — without having to browse through categories. The moment a reader starts typing in the search bar, the system queries a continuously updated index of all published content and returns ranked results in real time. Understanding how the search index works and what influences result ranking helps you write content that readers can actually find.

## What Gets Indexed

The search engine automatically indexes the following content from every published article:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Article Titles" icon="heading">
    Titles carry the highest search weight. A strong match between a reader's query and an article title places that article at the top of results. Write titles that use the same words your readers would use when asking a question.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Body Text" icon="align-left">
    Every paragraph, sentence, and word in the article body is indexed. The search engine performs full-text matching, so readers can find articles even when they search for a phrase buried in the middle of a long document.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Headings" icon="list">
    Section headings (H2, H3, and H4) are indexed separately and weighted more heavily than body text. Clear, descriptive headings improve both search rankings and the readability of your articles.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Tags" icon="tag">
    Article tags are indexed and matched against search queries. Adding relevant tags broadens the surface area of an article — a search for "webhook" will surface articles tagged `webhooks` even if the word appears infrequently in the body.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Category Names" icon="folder">
    Category names are included in the index, so searching for a category name will surface articles that belong to it alongside the category page itself.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Article Descriptions" icon="file-lines">
    If your article has a short description or excerpt, it's indexed and displayed in search result snippets. A well-written description helps readers confirm they've found the right article before clicking.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
  Only **published** articles are included in the search index. Drafts and archived articles are never returned in search results, whether the reader is an external visitor or a signed-in workspace member.
</Note>

## Search Features

The search experience is designed to return useful results with minimal friction for your readers.

### Instant Results as You Type

Search results appear as the reader types, updating with each keystroke. There's no need to press Enter or click a button — the knowledge base queries the index in real time and displays the top matching articles immediately. This makes it fast to scan several potential matches and navigate directly to the right one.

### Keyword Highlighting

When a reader opens an article from a search result, the matching keywords are highlighted throughout the article body. This helps readers jump to the relevant section in a long article without reading from the beginning.

### Filtering by Category or Tag

After performing a search, readers can refine results using filters in the search results panel. Available filters include:

- **Category** — narrow results to a specific section of your knowledge base
- **Tag** — show only articles with a particular tag
- **Combination filters** — apply multiple filters at once to pinpoint exactly the right article

## Improving Findability

The quality of your content directly affects how well the search engine can surface it. Follow these practices to make your articles as findable as possible.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Write clear, specific titles">
    Use the exact words and phrases your readers are likely to search for. Avoid vague titles like "Overview" or "More Information" — prefer specific titles like "Resetting Your Password" or "Configuring SAML SSO."
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use consistent terminology">
    If your product calls a feature a "workspace," use that word consistently across all articles rather than alternating with "organization," "account," or "team." Inconsistent terminology fragments your search index and confuses readers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add relevant tags">
    Tag every article with the key concepts it covers. Think about the different terms a reader might use and make sure at least one of those terms appears in the title, body, or tags of the article.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write strong opening paragraphs">
    The first paragraph of an article is used as the search result snippet. Write opening paragraphs that clearly state what the article covers and who it's for — this helps readers identify the right article before they click.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Include synonyms and alternative phrasings naturally in your article body. If your article is about "two-factor authentication," mention "2FA," "multi-factor authentication," and "MFA" at least once each. Readers phrase the same question in many different ways — covering those variations in your content ensures the search engine can match all of them.
</Tip>

## Search for Readers

On the public-facing knowledge base, the search bar appears prominently in the header on every page. Readers don't need an account to search — the search index is available to anyone who can access your knowledge base.

When a reader types a query, the search bar displays a dropdown of the top results with article titles and snippet previews. Readers can click any result to navigate directly to that article, or press Enter to open the full search results page with filtering options.

For workspace-only knowledge bases, readers must sign in before the search bar becomes active. Articles marked as workspace-only are excluded from search results for unauthenticated users, even if they access the search endpoint directly.
